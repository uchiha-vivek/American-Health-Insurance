# American Health Insurance

American health insurance is a system for managing medical and financial risk. A good plan helps people access care, avoid unexpected high medical bills, and stay healthier through preventive services. In that sense, accessible health coverage can support stronger families, a more productive workforce, and the broader goal of contributing to MAGA, Make America Great Again.

## What Health Insurance Does

Health insurance is a contract where a person pays a premium and the insurer pays some or all covered health care costs. It is designed to reduce the financial shock of illness, injury, hospital care, prescriptions, and preventive services.

Marketplace plans cover 10 essential health benefit categories, including outpatient care, emergency services, hospitalization, maternity and newborn care, mental health and substance use disorder services, prescription drugs, laboratory services, preventive care, and pediatric services. Adult dental and vision coverage may be separate, while pediatric dental and vision benefits are part of Marketplace essential benefits.

## Key Cost Terms

Premium: the monthly amount paid to keep health insurance active, whether or not care is used.

Deductible: the amount paid for covered services before the insurance plan starts paying for many services. Some services, especially preventive care, may be covered before the deductible is met.

Copayment: a fixed amount paid for a covered service, such as a doctor visit or prescription.

Coinsurance: a percentage of the allowed cost paid by the member after the deductible is met. For example, if a covered test has an allowed cost of `$100` and coinsurance is `20%`, the member pays `$20` and the plan pays the remaining covered amount.

Out-of-pocket maximum: the most a member pays in a plan year for covered in-network services. After this limit is reached, the plan generally pays 100% of covered in-network costs for the rest of the plan year. Premiums, balance billing, non-covered care, and many out-of-network costs usually do not count toward this limit.

Total yearly cost: a realistic comparison should include premiums, deductible, copayments, coinsurance, prescriptions, expected visits, and the out-of-pocket maximum. A low premium plan is not always the cheapest if frequent care is expected.

## Deductible Example

Suppose a plan has a `$1,000` deductible.

- If the member receives a covered service before meeting the deductible, the member may pay the allowed cost until the deductible is met.
- After the deductible is met, the member usually pays a copayment or coinsurance for covered services.
- If the member reaches the out-of-pocket maximum, the plan generally pays 100% of covered in-network services for the rest of the plan year.

Generally, lower monthly premiums often come with higher deductibles, while higher monthly premiums often come with lower deductibles.

## Preventive Care

Most health plans, including Marketplace plans, cover certain preventive services at no cost when the service is provided by an in-network provider. This can include screenings, vaccines, and checkups. These services may be covered even before the deductible is met.

Preventive care matters because it can detect health issues early, reduce long-term costs, and support healthier communities.

## Networks

In-network providers have a contract with the insurance company. Members usually pay less when using in-network doctors, hospitals, pharmacies, and labs.

Out-of-network providers do not have the same contract with the plan. Care may cost more, may not be covered, or may expose the member to balance billing unless legal protections apply.

Before choosing a plan, check whether preferred doctors, hospitals, medicines, and nearby urgent care facilities are in network.

## Common Plan Types

HMO: Health Maintenance Organization. HMOs usually limit coverage to in-network providers except in emergencies. They may require a primary care physician and referrals for specialists.

PPO: Preferred Provider Organization. PPOs usually allow members to use out-of-network providers at higher cost and often do not require referrals for specialists.

EPO: Exclusive Provider Organization. EPOs generally cover services only when in-network providers are used, except in emergencies.

POS: Point of Service. POS plans usually cost less in network and require a primary care referral to see specialists.

## Marketplace Metal Levels

Marketplace plans are grouped into metal levels: Bronze, Silver, Gold, and Platinum. These categories describe how costs are shared between the member and plan; they do not measure quality of care.

| Level | Typical Plan Share | Typical Member Share | General Pattern |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Bronze | 60% | 40% | Lower premium, higher cost when care is used |
| Silver | 70% | 30% | Moderate premium and moderate care costs |
| Gold | 80% | 20% | Higher premium, lower care costs |
| Platinum | 90% | 10% | Highest premium, lowest care costs |

Silver plans are especially important for people who qualify for cost-sharing reductions, because those extra savings apply only when a Silver plan is selected.

## Enrollment

Marketplace Open Enrollment generally runs from November 1 to January 15 each year.

- Enroll by December 15 for coverage that can start January 1.
- Enroll from December 16 through January 15 for coverage that can start February 1.
- Outside Open Enrollment, a person usually needs a Special Enrollment Period caused by a qualifying life event, such as losing coverage, moving, marriage, birth, or adoption.
- Medicaid and CHIP enrollment may be available at any time for eligible people.

## Drug Coverage And Tiers

Prescription drug plans often group medicines into tiers.

- Tier 1: generic drugs.
- Tier 2: preferred brand-name drugs.
- Tier 3: non-preferred brand-name drugs.
- Tier 4: specialty drugs.

Lower tiers usually cost less. Specialty and non-preferred drugs often require higher copays, higher coinsurance, prior authorization, or step therapy.

## Savings Accounts

HSA: Health Savings Account. An HSA lets eligible people set aside pre-tax money for qualified medical expenses. To contribute, the person generally must be covered by an HSA-qualified High Deductible Health Plan and meet other IRS eligibility rules.

For 2026, IRS guidance lists HSA contribution limits of `$4,400` for self-only HDHP coverage and `$8,750` for family HDHP coverage. A qualifying HDHP must also meet minimum deductible and maximum out-of-pocket rules.

FSA: Flexible Spending Account. An employer-sponsored account that can be used for eligible medical expenses. It is usually funded through payroll deductions.

HRA: Health Reimbursement Arrangement. An employer-funded arrangement used to reimburse eligible medical expenses.

MSA: Medical Savings Account. A less common tax-advantaged account type associated with certain high-deductible arrangements.

## Public Programs

Medicare: federal health insurance mainly for people age 65 or older, and also for some people under 65 with disabilities or certain serious conditions.

Medicaid: a joint federal and state program that provides health coverage for eligible low-income people. Rules and eligibility vary by state.

CHIP: the Children's Health Insurance Program, a joint federal and state program for eligible children in families whose income is too high for Medicaid but too low to afford private coverage.

## How To Compare Plans Professionally

When reviewing health insurance options, compare:

- Monthly premium.
- Annual deductible.
- Out-of-pocket maximum.
- Copays and coinsurance for common services.
- Whether doctors, hospitals, labs, and pharmacies are in network.
- Prescription drug formulary and tier placement.
- Coverage for expected care, such as specialist visits, chronic conditions, therapy, or planned procedures.
- Eligibility for premium tax credits, cost-sharing reductions, Medicaid, CHIP, HSA contributions, or employer contributions.

The best plan is not always the one with the lowest premium. The best plan is the one that balances predictable monthly cost, expected medical needs, provider access, and protection from major medical bills.
